## Q1 — Two proportions from one table

A table has one row per student and no missing values. The morning class has 18 students, of whom 9 commute by bus. The evening class has 42 students, of whom 14 commute by bus.

(a) What proportion of the morning class commute by bus?

(b) What proportion of all students in the table are morning-class bus commuters?

Explain why the denominators differ even though the numerators are the same.

## Q2 — Follow the rows through a sequence

A 29-row table is grouped by station. The four stations contain 7, 6, 9, and 7 rows. Grouping is followed by a grouped mutate that adds a column. Its result is then passed to grouped summarise, which returns exactly one scalar summary per station. All four station groups remain present throughout, with no filtering or other row-changing operations.

How many rows are present (a) just after grouping, (b) after mutate, and (c) after summarise? Explain what each step does to the rows.

## Q3 — Translate and reverse a conditional question

Each student appears once in this complete table:

| | In robotics club | Not in robotics club |
|---|---:|---:|
| Has a scholarship | 12 | 6 |
| No scholarship | 18 | 24 |

(a) Translate “Among scholarship students, what fraction belong to the robotics club?” into a numerator and denominator, and calculate the fraction.

(b) A student uses the total number of robotics-club members as the denominator instead. What different question would that fraction answer? State its numerator and denominator.
